EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Executive Summary provides the reader with a high-level orientation to how employment law functions in England and Wales. It explains the professional field, identifies why the area matters in practice, and places the later sections in context by linking statutory rights, contracts, dismissal, redundancy, ACAS procedure and cross-border relevance into one coherent overview.

Employment law in England and Wales regulates the legal framework governing hiring, employment conditions, workplace rights, management obligations and the ending of employment relationships. It is not limited to dismissal disputes. It also covers employment status, written statements of employment particulars, pay, working time, annual leave, family leave, discrimination, workplace policies, grievance and disciplinary processes, redundancy, settlement agreements and Employment Tribunal claims. The field is relevant both to domestic employers and to foreign businesses that hire staff, establish operations, acquire companies or restructure workforces in England and Wales.

The main statutory framework includes the Employment Rights Act 1996, Equality Act 2010, Working Time Regulations 1998, National Minimum Wage Act 1998, Employment Relations Act 1999, Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992 and the evolving Employment Rights Act 2025. A proper analysis usually requires parallel assessment of statutory law, the employment contract, staff handbook, workplace practice, collective arrangements, ACAS guidance, service length and the procedural record.

Annual leave is a core statutory right. Workers receive 5.6 weeks of paid holiday in each leave year, generally capped at 28 days for someone working five days weekly. Employers can include public holidays within that entitlement if the contract makes this clear. From 6 April 2026, employers must keep records showing compliance with annual leave and holiday pay requirements for at least six years. [web:529][web:535]

Dismissal and redundancy require careful legal and procedural analysis. Ordinary unfair dismissal generally currently requires two years of service, while automatically unfair dismissal and discrimination claims do not. Notice and redundancy payments are service-based, and most claims require ACAS early conciliation before an Employment Tribunal claim. For relevant events on or after 1 October 2026, most Tribunal claim periods increase to six months minus one day; earlier events normally retain the three-month-minus-one-day position. [web:529][web:530][web:531][web:532][web:533] Employment law in England and Wales is therefore a central professional function for employers, HR teams, investors, legal advisers and internationally active businesses seeking compliant and predictable workforce management.
